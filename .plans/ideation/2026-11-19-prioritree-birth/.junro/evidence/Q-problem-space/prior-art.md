## 1. PSYCHOMETRICS — corpus-knowledge at discovery (validated: A1 CONFIRMED, A2 CORRECTED)

Key result. Thurstone (1927) founded scale construction from pairwise comparison alone: judges cannot report the absolute magnitude of a sensation, but they reliably report discriminal differences between two stimuli. From the proportion p_ij of "i judged greater" across judges, a latent scale is constructed — Case V: S_i − S_j = z(p_ij)·√2σ. This is the product's stated premise almost verbatim — the basketball/grapefruit sentence is Thurstone's founding move, with "size of sensation" generalized to "any comparison operator." The method descends from Fechner's psychophysics (1860, Elemente der Psychophysik).

Adaptive Comparative Judgment (ACJ). Pollitt & Murray (1996); Pollitt (2012). In educational assessment, absolute rubric-based marking of open-ended work (essays, portfolios) is chronically unreliable across markers; ACJ replaces it with pairwise judgments adaptively scheduled (an Elo-like algorithm presents items of estimated-similar merit, maximizing information per judgment). This is the closest human-judgment precedent for the entire product thesis, at production scale in e-assessment.

Interface shape. Pairwise judgments from multiple judges → per-pair win proportions → latent scale with error terms. Output: not just an order — an interval scale with separable uncertainty.

Implication for the Problem Space. The core state transition has a 99-year-old, empirically validated form: votes in → latent scale out. The tally's output shape should include scale positions + error, not only a permutation. "Comparison operator" as a parameter is a genuine generalization beyond Thurstone (who fixed the operator to "greater on the sensed continuum") — and is where prior art thins out.

## 2. PROBABILISTIC RANKING MODELS — corpus-knowledge at discovery (validated: B5-B7 CONFIRMED)

Key result. Zermelo (1929) and Bradley & Terry (1952) — P(i beats j) = π_i/(π_i + π_j) (logit form: σ(β_i − β_j)) — infer latent strengths from batch maximum-likelihood over pairwise outcomes, natively handling incomplete designs (BT's title is literally "Rank Analysis of Incomplete Block Designs"). Undefeated items have infinite MLE (needs an anchor or Bayesian prior — a real edge case the tally must own). Luce (1959) + Plackett (1975) generalize to full ranked votes (Plackett-Luce); Mallows (1957) is the standard noise model over rankings (Kendall-tau-centered).

Elo (1978) — the same logistic model but updated incrementally per game: R' = R + K(S − E). Order-dependent: the final rating depends on the sequence of games, not just the multiset. Glicko/Glicko-2 (Glickman 1999): adds per-player rating deviation (RD) — the system computes what it doesn't know: new/uncertain players move fast, established ones barely. TrueSkill (Herbrich, Minka & Graepel, NIPS 2006): Bayesian factor-graph skill rating — converges on far fewer games than Elo.

Interface shape. Noisy pairwise outcomes → (score, uncertainty) per item; order = sort by score; honesty = CIs/overlap reported.

Implication for the Problem Space. "The tally is a pure function of the stored votes" (README.md:5) commits the product to the Bradley-Terry side of this family, not the Elo side — batch MLE over the whole vote log is order-independent and replayable; Elo's incremental updates are not. And a noise-aware tally can honestly output what no raw win-count can: confidence that the current order is right, which is what makes a scheduler's stop condition possible and what "superior order" must mean statistically.

## 3. SOCIAL CHOICE / RANK AGGREGATION — corpus-knowledge at discovery (validated: A4-A7; A6 CORRECTED)

Key results. Condorcet (1785): pairwise majorities can cycle (A>B, B>C, C>A) — the aggregate of even perfectly honest votes can contain no consistent global ranking; the Condorcet winner (beats all pairwise) may not exist. Borda (1781): positional points, ignores pairwise structure. Copeland (1951): pairwise win counts. Kemeny (1959) / Young & Levenglick (1978): the ranking minimizing total pairwise disagreement — the median ranking — and Young (1988) showed Kemeny is exactly the maximum-likelihood estimator of a "true" ranking under a certain noise model (social choice and statistics converge here). Bartholdi, Tovey & Trick (1989): Kemeny is NP-hard — the "best" tally is computationally infeasible at scale. Practical Condorcet-consistent polynomial tallies: Ranked Pairs (Tideman 1987), Schulze beatpaths (Schulze 2011) — used in real elections (Debian, Wikimedia). Arrow (1951): for ≥3 alternatives, no aggregation satisfies unrestricted domain + Pareto + IIA + non-dictatorship simultaneously — the axiomatic ceiling: there is no "perfect" tally; every tally is a priced choice of which axiom to sacrifice. Gibbard (1973)/Satterthwaite (1975): every non-dictatorial scheme over ≥3 outcomes is manipulable by strategic voters.

Interface shape. n² win-matrix (or per-judge rankings) → a voting rule → a total order; each rule resolves cycles by a priced policy (Kemeny: minimal disagreement; Ranked Pairs: lock strongest majorities first; BT: fit a transitive model and let the model absorb cycles).

Implication for the Problem Space. This is the domain that bounds the claim. Concretely: cycles are a named input-entropy class (Q-problem-space's own vocabulary), not a bug; the tally's cycle-resolution policy must be an explicit design ruling; BT-style tallies enforce transitivity by modeling assumption and sacrifice IIA (adding a new item rescales everyone — the ruled purpose accepts this by construction); and "superior order" must be stated as a comparative empirical claim, never an axiomatic optimality claim.

## 4. LLM-AS-JUDGE — corpus-knowledge at discovery (validated: B1-B2 CORRECTED, B3-B4 CONFIRMED)

Key results. Chatbot Arena / LMSYS (Zheng et al. 2023, "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena," NeurIPS 2023 D&B; Chiang et al. 2024, "Chatbot Arena: An Open Platform…," ICML 2024) — the closest live precedent for the product: millions of pairwise human votes ("which response is better?") stored → Bradley-Terry/Elo leaderboard of 100+ models, with confidence intervals displayed and later "style control" regressions against length/markdown (2024). MT-Bench (same paper): GPT-4 as pairwise judge reaches ~80%+ agreement with human preference — comparable to or exceeding inter-human agreement (~66%) — and pairwise comparison mode substantially beats single-answer absolute grading on agreement.

Documented judge biases (the LLM comparator's input-entropy classes): position/order bias — verdicts flip when the two candidates swap presentation order (Zheng et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2023, "Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators") → mitigation: ask both orders, average; verbosity bias — longer answers win more (Zheng et al. 2023; the reason AlpacaEval 2.0 introduced length-controlled win rates — Dubois et al. 2024); self-enhancement bias — judges favor their own model family (evidence mixed across studies); self-inconsistency — same pair, different verdict across samples/temperatures.

Interface shape. Two candidate texts (+context/instruction) → verdict A/B/tie, often with chain-of-thought-before-verdict to stabilize.

Implication for the Problem Space. The LLM half of the comparator class is not an unbiased oracle — it has measurable, named bias fields. The product's premise ("discernment is easy for LLMs") is confirmed for pairwise mode specifically (absolute scoring is where LLM judges degrade) — a sharper support than the ruling's simplification. The vote schema must carry the data that makes these biases detectable and mitigable (presentation order, judge identity), and the ruled phrase "running through the pairwise permutations" reads — correctly — as ordered pairs: both presentations, not one.

## 5. NOISY SORTING / ACTIVE RANKING — corpus-knowledge at discovery (validated: B8-B11 CORRECTED, claims confirmed in substance)

Key results. Feige, Raghavan, Peleg & Upfal (1994), "Computing with Noisy Queries" — sorting with erroneous comparisons: repeating each comparison (majority vote) restores high-probability sorting at a logarithmic factor overhead. Braverman & Mossel (~2008-09, "Noisy Sorting," SODA) — recurrent errors (same pair always answers the same wrong way, modeling biased judges) still allow near-exact ranking recovery with O(n log n) comparisons up to a few misplaced items. Jamieson & Nowak (2011), "Active Ranking using Pairwise Comparisons" (ICML) — the adaptive regime: choose each next pair from past outcomes; with items embedded in R^d, the maximum item (and with extensions, near-full rankings) is identified with exponentially fewer comparisons than the full n² round-robin, with PAC-style guarantees. Dueling bandits (Yue, Broder, Kleinberg & Joachims 2012, "The K-armed Dueling Bandits Problem," IEEE TPAMI 34(1); earlier Yue & Joachims ICML 2009) — best-arm/Condorcet-winner identification from noisy pairwise feedback under a budget — this is the theory of pair scheduling.

The scale arithmetic (tool-verified, this packet): full pairwise = n(n−1)/2 unordered pairs; ordered permutations (both presentations) = n(n−1): n=10 → 90 ordered votes · n=100 → 9,900 · n=1000 → 999,000. At 10 s per human judgment, n=1000's full both-order sweep = 9,990,000 s = 2,775 hours = 115.625 days of continuous judging; even the unordered half (499,500 pairs) is infeasible for humans at n=1000.

Interface shape. Budgeted comparator oracle → adaptive policy picks pairs → stop when posterior confidence ≥ threshold.

Implication for the Problem Space. "Pair scheduling" being a named core part (README.md:15) is exactly what this literature says must exist: full sweeps are a gold standard for small n and verification runs, while the usable product at scale requires uncertainty-driven adaptive scheduling (ask pairs whose current rating CIs overlap — the ACJ/Elo-matchmaking move), with termination as a first-class terminal state (done = confidence reached within budget; safe halt = budget exhausted → partial order with explicit unknowns).

## 6. STORAGE PRECEDENT (brief) — corpus-knowledge at discovery

Key results. Event sourcing (Fowler 2005, the "Event Sourcing" bliki): append-only events are the system of record; state is a replay — corrections are compensating events, never edits. CQRS (Young 2010): the write model (append events) and read model (projection) separate — the tally is literally a CQRS read model projected from the vote event stream, which is what "pure function of the stored votes" says in this vocabulary. Tamper-evident logs: Schneier & Kelsey (1999) hash-chained secure audit logs; Certificate Transparency (RFC 6962, 2013; RFC 9162, 2021) Merkle-tree append-only public logs; git itself (Torvalds 2005) as content-addressed immutable history.

In-tree precedent (disk-verified): this tree already runs exactly these ledger semantics in its own friction engine — .opencode/scripts/friction_ledger.py ("a duplicate proposal is not a second vote"): append-only, corrections are new entries referencing the old handle, voids retire numbers permanently.

Implication for the Problem Space. The vote store's shape is the least novel part of the product — a well-trodden append-only event log with hash chaining; the family's own ledger idiom is a ready pattern. Deterministic replay (same log → same ranking) is the storage-side guarantee the pure-tally ruling already demands.

## SYNTHESIS

### (a) ECHOES — what the repo's stated mechanics already re-invent (name by name)

1. The Thurstone echo — README.md:7's basketball/grapefruit is Thurstone (1927)'s founding premise: absolute magnitude unmeasurable, discriminal differences observable. Priori-tree is a generalized Thurstone scale constructor, with the comparison operator as a parameter.
2. The Bradley-Terry/Zermelo echo — "the tally is a pure function of the stored votes" (README.md:5) is the batch-MLE side of the rating family (Zermelo 1929, Bradley-Terry 1952), explicitly not Elo's order-dependent incremental updates — the ruling accidentally (or deliberately) picked the replayable, order-independent semantics.
3. The Chatbot-Arena echo — the product's pipeline (pairwise votes in → deterministic tally → ranked leaderboard out) is Chatbot Arena's pipeline (Zheng et al. 2023; Chiang et al. 2024) with both axes generalized: Arena's items are model responses and its operator is fixed ("better response"); Priori-tree parameterizes both, and fuses Arena's human voters with MT-Bench's LLM judge through one vote interface (README.md:5).
4. The complete-paired-design echo — "running through the pairwise permutations" (README.md:3/7) is the classical complete paired-comparison design (David 1963) — and the word "permutations" (ordered pairs) accidentally pre-encodes the both-presentation protocol that LLM order bias demands (§4).
5. The event-sourcing echo — "votes are data in/out, no DB at birth; pure-function tally" (README.md:15) is CQRS/event sourcing exactly: append-only vote log = write model, ranking = derived read model — and the tree's own friction ledger already implements the correction/void semantics a vote ledger needs.
6. The active-ranking echo — "pair scheduling" named as a first-class core part (README.md:15) is the slot where Jamieson-Nowak / dueling bandits / ACJ theory plugs in: prior art says the scheduler is a designed policy with optimality guarantees, not a loop.

### (b) SOUND — where prior art validates the premise

- Thurstone 1927 + a century of psychometric practice: pairwise-comparison scaling builds reliable scales precisely where absolute judgment fails — the premise is the field's founding result, not a hypothesis.
- ACJ (Pollitt 2012 et al.): in real assessment deployments, adaptive pairwise judgment outperforms absolute rubric marking on reliability (~0.9 vs ~0.6-0.8, corpus-approximate) — empirical production evidence for "superior order than sizing individual items" among human judges.
- Zheng et al. 2023 (MT-Bench): for LLM judges, pairwise mode beats single-answer absolute grading on human agreement (~80%+, comparable to inter-human) — the premise holds for the LLM comparator specifically in the pairwise regime the product chose.
- Chatbot Arena at scale: millions of noisy pairwise human votes → a stable, widely trusted ranking of 100+ models — the exact store-votes-tally-rank pipeline demonstrably works end-to-end in production, noise and all.

### (c) BOUNDS — what "superior order" can and cannot mean

- Arrow (1951) — the axiomatic ceiling: no tally over ≥3 items is simultaneously unrestricted-domain + Pareto + IIA + non-dictatorial. "Superior" can never mean "the uniquely rational aggregate." BT-style tallies additionally impose a transitive latent scale by assumption and sacrifice IIA (a new item rescales the old ones). The tally is a priced choice, and the price list should be in the design doc.
- Condorcet (1785) — cycles exist: even perfectly honest votes can produce a majority cycle; a total order can be unwarranted by the data. Cycles are a named input-entropy class; the tally needs an explicit, documented resolution policy (Kemeny / Ranked Pairs / BT-model-absorption each price it differently).
- Bartholdi-Tovey-Trick (1989): the "best" tally (Kemeny = the MLE ranking) is NP-hard — at scale every honest tally is an approximation; small-n exact and large-n approximate should be named as two modes.
- Gibbard-Satterthwaite (1973/75): non-dictatorial schemes are strategically manipulable — with LLM judges, "strategy" means prompt gaming; bound on trusting any single judge.
- LLM-judge biases (Zheng et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2023; Dubois et al. 2024): position, verbosity, self-enhancement, self-inconsistency — the LLM comparator's error model is structured, not white noise; "superior" is bounded by the measured bias rates, and the vote schema must carry enough metadata to measure them.
- Noise statistics (Glicko/TrueSkill): from noisy votes, the honest output of any tally is (estimate, uncertainty) — a distribution over rankings, not an order. Therefore the defensible claim is the comparative one: pairwise + tally beats absolute sizing at equal budget — an empirical claim with a measurable acceptance criterion. The undefensible one: that the output order is "correct."

### (d) CONCRETE IMPLICATIONS (5)

1. Vote schema — append-only, hash-chained vote records (friction-ledger semantics: corrections = compensating votes, never edits); fields: item_a, item_b, winner{a|b|tie}, presentation_order, judge_class{human|llm}, judge_id, operator_id, timestamp[, judge_confidence]. presentation_order is non-optional (it is the anti-order-bias datum); ties/abstentions first-class; votes scoped to an operator.
2. Tally design — default tally = Bradley-Terry MLE (= Thurstone Case V up to link function): noise-aware, native to incomplete vote sets, deterministic and replayable (honors the purity ruling), with a Bayesian prior/anchor to handle undefeated-item infinite MLE. Output = (rank, score, uncertainty) per item plus diagnostics: Condorcet-winner existence, cycle count, order-swap flip rate. Exact Kemeny as a small-n/audit mode (NP-hard wall named); BT for scale.
3. Pair scheduler — two named modes: (i) complete sweep (both permutations — the ruling's literal design) for small n and verification runs; (ii) adaptive for scale: uncertainty-driven (next pair = the two items with overlapping rating CIs — the ACJ/Elo-matchmaking/Jamieson-Nowak move), budget-aware, terminating on posterior confidence over adjacent ranks. Tool-verified motivation: at n=1000 the complete both-order sweep is 999,000 judgments ≈ 115.625 days at 10 s each — adaptive scheduling is the difference between a usable product and an unusable one.
4. Acceptance criteria — make "superior order" measurable: (i) test-retest: split the vote log, tally each half, require Kendall-tau agreement ≥ threshold; (ii) cross-class: tally(human-only) vs tally(LLM-only) vs tally(all) agreement measured, never assumed; (iii) order-swap flip rate for LLM judges below a threshold (position-bias bound); (iv) synthetic ground truth: generate items from a known BT latent scale, vote with noise p, require bounded recovery error — the family's zero-and-zero style as a deterministic golden test; (v) cycle rate reported as data quality, never hidden; (vi) budget curve: adaptive vs random vs full-sweep, accuracy-per-vote.
5. Judge-class weighting is a priced future ruling, not a schema fork — one vote interface (README.md:5) means one schema; if measured reliability differs (LLM biases vs human noise), reliability-weighted BT (TrueSkill-style per-judge factors) is the mechanism — a tally parameter, ruled later on the measured cross-class agreement data, not a structural difference.
