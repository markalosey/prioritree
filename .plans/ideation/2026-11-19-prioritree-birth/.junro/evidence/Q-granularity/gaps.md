## 2. The ruling-shaped gap questions (the collaborator could answer each with one ruling)

1. What exactly is a voting round's state machine — when does a round open and close, what is held between pair presentations, and may a round pause and resume?
2. Which problem-side constraints are hard for the core — is every pair judgeable (or may a comparator refuse one), are comparisons costly enough to schedule around, and must the comparison operator's outcome be binary?
3. What entropy enters at the inputs — malformed, duplicate, or empty items; ambiguous or unanswerable pairs; fatigued or inconsistent comparators — and which of these does the core tolerate versus refuse with a named error?
4. Which product failure modes are irreversible — can a wrong or mischievous vote ever be unmade, and can a tally run corrupt the votes it reads?
5. What are the exact binary terminal states — under what conditions does a run end "done" (a ranked whole returned) versus "safe halt," and what does a safe halt return?
6. Is "any list and any comparison operator" fenced at birth — a maximum item count, a required operator contract, and a minimum an item must carry to be comparable?
7. Are "items, comparison operator, pair scheduling, votes, tally" the core's five atomic units, and what acceptance test closes each unit's gate?
8. Which acts are ruled stochastic versus deterministic — is pair scheduling deterministic code, is vote validation deterministic, and is the vote elicitation the ONLY stochastic act in the chain?
9. What is the vote interface's exact narrowest shape — the least set of fields a vote must carry (pair identity, outcome, comparator, operator, round?) and nothing more?
10. What exactly IS a vote — its fields, their types, and its identity: what makes two votes distinct, and what makes a re-read vote the same vote?
11. Do errant votes exist in the model, and what tolerance is ruled — is noise handled by redundancy (more votes per pair), by the tally's aggregation rule, or not handled at birth?
12. When a comparator judges two items equal or declines to judge, what is recorded — a third vote outcome, an abstention record, or a refusal — and how does the tally count it?
13. May the same comparator vote the same pair more than once, and if so does the last vote supersede, the first hold, or all count?
14. Under what coverage condition is the tally ALLOWED to fire — the full pairwise permutation, a quorum threshold, or any partial coverage — and what happens to pairs never voted?
15. What acceptance test decides an output ordering is "superior" — agreement with a ground-truth ordering, improvement over an absolute-sizing baseline, stability under vote resampling — and is any measurable criterion ruled at all?
16. When stored votes form a cycle (a>b, b>c, c>a), what does the tally do — break it by a deterministic rule, report it as a named tie class, or refuse the tally?
17. Human and LLM vote through one interface — but do their votes carry the same weight, is the comparator's identity recorded or anonymous, and does one vote per comparator per pair bind?
18. Is the item set immutable once a round opens — and if an item is added, removed, or edited mid-round, does the round restart, refuse, or continue with the change named?
19. Can a cast vote be revoked or corrected, and if the family's append-only school holds (corrections are new entries referencing the old), does the tally read the correction chain's end state?
20. What is the ranking's output contract — a total order, a weak order with ties allowed, or a partial order with unrelated items named — and what does the core return when the votes cannot decide?
21. What scale bounds fence the core at birth — a maximum item count the pairwise permutations must serve within what budget, and what does the core do when a list exceeds the bound?
22. Does the vote store inherit the FR-MEM-0143 class (parallel writes silently clobbering) — at which tier are concurrent votes serialized, and is the core's statelessness the ruled whole answer at birth?
23. Is the tally's pure-function ruling the whole idempotency story — are tally re-runs byte-identical by ruling, and what is the stance on a vote written twice?

## 3. The purpose-statement verdict — does it pass the article's own solution-neutrality test?

Verdict: NO — it fails the strict test, though it fails gracefully. The ruled purpose is tool-neutral but not mechanism-neutral: it embeds the solution inside the "what." The evidence, verbatim (junro.json:429, the collaborator's Q-purpose ruling): "Priori-tree is a generalized prioritization system based on the concept that LLM and Humans are great at discerning the difference between two items but not great at sizing an item. That is a simplification. Consider: a human is great at determining that a basketball is larger than a grapefruit. But not great as identifying the exact dimensions of a grapefruit or a basketball. By taking a list and running through the pairwise permutations for any given comparision operator. Storing the votes, then tallying. we get a list out that is in a superior order than trying to size individual items."

Problem-side content (passes): the measurement premise — discernment between two items is easy, absolute sizing is not — and the desired outcome, a superior ordering. No tools, storage, languages, or frameworks are named anywhere. Solution-side content (fails): "run the pairwise permutations… Storing the votes, then tallying" is the solution — a specific algorithm family stated as the purpose itself; and the ruling's codicil goes further into implementation: "both vote through the same vote interface; the tally is a pure function of the stored votes" (junro.json:429) — an interface commitment and a determinism property inside the purpose record. The goal line fuses both in one breath: "To produce superior orderings from pairwise comparison…" (AGENTS.md:4) — the outcome and the method in five words. The repo's own record acknowledges the retrofit: all three newly-opened questions restate this mechanism as ground and then ask for the solution-neutral derivation after the fact. The derivation's honesty will depend on separating the premise (problem) from the pairwise-vote-tally (solution) — and on treating the codicil's commitments (one interface, pure-function tally) as constraints the solution must honor, not as parts of the problem.
